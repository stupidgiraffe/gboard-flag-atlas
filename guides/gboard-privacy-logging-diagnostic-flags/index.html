Gboard Privacy and Diagnostic Flags: What to Avoid Enabling

Identify flags that may affect diagnostic data, audio history and training caches, and learn how to evaluate privacy implications.

What you should know

A diagnostic flag name is not a privacy guarantee. Avoid collecting sensitive typing or speech data without an explicit reason.

Evidence standard: these are real catalog identifiers, but their effects can be inferred, version-specific, or gated. Check the confidence and APK notes before changing settings.

Logging and feature behavior aren't interchangeable

enable_internal_speech_enhancement_pii_logging indicates logging connected to speech-enhancement internals, not a normal feature to enable casually. enable_save_audio_history_to_file suggests persistence of audio-related data. enable_logging_for_emoji_search_query likewise concerns traces that could reveal what was typed or searched. These are especially poor candidates for blanket 'recommended tweaks' lists.

Local caches have tradeoffs

Gboard includes references to user-feature caches, training caches and locally aggregated metrics. A flag's presence does not tell us the exact retention period, where data is stored, whether encryption is used or which diagnostic endpoint might receive a report. Even an 'on-device' name does not certify the absence of all network activity.

Use normal privacy controls

Prefer supported Gboard privacy and personalization settings, avoid enabling PII/audio logging, and review permissions with Android settings when investigating behavior. If you need evidence for the Atlas, inspect code paths in an APK or use synthetic test inputs. Don't upload a private real-world cache or speech recording just to decipher a flag.

Frequently asked questions

Does disabling a logging flag stop all telemetry?

No. One identifier is not a complete audit of every diagnostics and network path.

Is exporting the UFC cache safe?

Its exported fields and destination are unverified. Treat the data as potentially sensitive.
